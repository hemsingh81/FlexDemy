Momentum

Level 1 · Simplest Level 2 Level 3 Level 4 Level 5

Momentum is a way of measuring how hard it is to stop something that's moving. A heavier object moving at the same speed as a lighter one has more momentum — and so does the same object moving faster.

This connects directly to

Inertia
An object's tendency to resist a change in its motion — a still object stays still, a moving object keeps moving, unless something pushes or pulls on it.
: a moving object "wants" to keep moving in a straight line at the same speed, and momentum is what tells you how much force it will take to change that.

In formula terms: momentum (p) = mass (m) × velocity (v). Not clicking for you yet? Keep reading below, or try a different way to hear it.

Way 3 — The car-crash way Picture two cars hitting a wall at the same speed: an empty hatchback and a fully loaded truck. The truck does far more damage, even though both were going the same speed — because it has more mass, it has more momentum, and momentum is exactly what determines how much force it takes to bring something to a stop.
Worked example: A 1,200 kg car and a 20,000 kg loaded truck both travel at 15 m/s. Car momentum = 1,200 × 15 = 18,000 kg·m/s. Truck momentum = 20,000 × 15 = 300,000 kg·m/s — over 16× more, which is why the truck needs a far longer stopping distance at the same speed.
