# PRD Quality Review — Dashboard (Role-Aware Merge of Dashboard + Tutor Hub & Booking)

## Overall verdict
This is a tight, well-grounded restructuring PRD: its every code and doc reference (`features/Dashboard/`, `features/TutorHub/`, `DEFAULT_VISIBLE_TABS`, the perspective-switcher in `TutorHubView.tsx`, the four mock services, `FRONTEND_PRD.md` §4.3/§4.6) checks out against the actual repo, and the thesis (one role-routed home, no manual toggle) is carried consistently from Vision through FRs to Success Metrics. The main soft spot is Done-ness clarity: 12 of 19 FRs (mostly the "carried over unchanged" ones in §4.2, §4.4, §4.5) have no explicit testable consequences, leaning entirely on SM-1's blanket "zero regressions" claim to cover acceptance. A secondary mechanical issue: the Assumptions Index round-trips only 1 of 5 entries to an inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag. Neither is severe for an internal, capability-already-exists PRD at this stakes level, but both are worth a pass before this feeds story creation.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The PRD states real decisions rather than hedging them: FR-2's "no manual perspective/preview switch is presented to Student or Tutor roles" is an unambiguous choice, and FR-3 carves out a named, narrower exception for Master/Support with an explicit rationale (they aren't registered tutors, so no real data). §9's Assumptions Index is honest about provenance — it distinguishes "Confirmed with stakeholder during Discovery," "Resolved with stakeholder in this review round," and "Decided by builder per stakeholder's 'decide yourself'" — which is exactly the kind of traceability that lets a reader see which calls were the PM's vs. the builder's.

The trade-off in §5's `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` ("a shared visual pass across both role dashboards is worth doing later but isn't required to ship this merge") names what's given up, not just what's chosen. §8 Open Questions is empty, which is credible given the doc's framing as a "review round" that folded prior resolutions in — but it also means there's no visible record of what was contested before landing here; a reader arriving cold has to reconstruct the tension from §9 rather than see it argued in §8.

### Findings
- **low** Open Questions section is a placeholder, not a record (§8) — "None outstanding as of this review round" gives no visibility into what was debated before the resolutions in §9 were reached (e.g., the Master/Support toggle design, or the "decide yourself" backend-ownership call). A reader can't independently judge whether those resolutions were well-reasoned. *Fix:* briefly note what each §9 resolution was resolving, either in §8 or inline at the resolution site.

## Substance over theater — strong
No theater detected. Two personas (Priya, Raj), both minimal and both driving specific FR groups (UJ-1 → FR-4 through FR-14; UJ-2 → FR-15 through FR-19) — not decorative. The Vision statement (§1) is concrete to this product, not swappable: it names the actual leftover artifact being removed ("a tab that also carries a manual 'preview the other role' switch left over from early development"). This was verified against `FrontEnd/src/features/TutorHub/TutorHubView.tsx` lines 30–31 and 44–69, which do contain exactly this — a local `perspective` state toggle between "Student View" and "Educator Studio" unrelated to the authenticated user's real role. No NFR boilerplate ("must be scalable/secure/reliable") appears anywhere — the PRD correctly omits an NFR section for a frontend-only routing merge rather than padding one in.

## Strategic coherence — strong
Clear thesis: eliminate the two-home split and the fake role-preview toggle by routing one nav entry off real `UserProfile.role`. Feature grouping follows the thesis (§4.1 shell/routing is the mechanism; §4.2–§4.5 are the content being routed) rather than reading as an arbitrary backlog. Success Metrics fit an internal restructuring: SM-1 (parity, zero regressions) and SM-2 (nav tab count 7→6) are operational, not vanity metrics like DAU — appropriate given the thesis is about consolidation, not engagement. SM-2's "7 top-level tabs to 6" was checked against `App.tsx`'s `DEFAULT_VISIBLE_TABS` (dashboard, discover, tutor, groups, assignments, certificates, admin = 7) and is arithmetically correct once `tutor` merges into `dashboard`. SM-C1 (role purity) is a genuine counter-metric that pressures against SM-2's simplification impulse, not a token one.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
This is the dimension most likely to cause friction downstream. Of the 19 FRs, only FR-1, FR-2, FR-3, FR-10, FR-14, and FR-15 carry an explicit **Consequences (testable)** block. The other 12 — FR-4 through FR-9, FR-11 through FR-13, and FR-16 through FR-19 — are single-sentence capability statements with no stated acceptance condition of their own. Some of this is mitigated by specificity baked into the FR text itself (FR-5 names the exact stat cards — Day Streak, Enrolled Courses, Mastery Points, Time Spent — which was confirmed to match `Dashboard.tsx` verbatim), but others are genuinely open to interpretation at build time: FR-8's "sort options (recently accessed / completion % / alphabetical)" has no stated behavior for ties or default sort; FR-16's "chart (monthly earnings, teaching hours logged, student engagement index)" has no stated bounds (what counts toward "engagement index," what happens with zero data); FR-17's "grid of available/booked slots (hover shows student + topic)" doesn't say what a Public Live Masterclass slot looks like differently from a 1-on-1 slot in that same grid.

The PRD's implicit answer is "match existing behavior exactly," backed by SM-1's "zero regressions against current behavior" — which is a legitimate acceptance strategy for a pure carry-over, but it's never stated as the acceptance criterion *at the FR level*, so a story-writer working FR-by-FR won't necessarily know to go verify against the live app rather than trust the FR's prose as the full spec.

### Findings
- **medium** Twelve of nineteen FRs lack a Consequences block (§4.2 FR-4–FR-8, §4.3 FR-9/FR-11/FR-12/FR-13, §4.4 FR-16/FR-17, §4.5 FR-18/FR-19) — no per-FR testable condition; acceptance is implied only by the blanket SM-1 parity metric. *Fix:* either add a one-line "Consequences: matches current `<Component>` behavior exactly (see `<file>`)" to each carried-over FR, or state once in §4's preamble that all FRs without an explicit Consequences block inherit "byte-for-byte behavioral parity with the current implementation" as their acceptance criterion, and point each FR at the source component so parity is checkable at all.
- **low** FR-16's "student engagement index" and FR-8's sort-tie behavior are undefined even by reference to existing code (§4.4 FR-16, §4.2 FR-8) — unlike other FRs, these don't name a source file the way FR-5 implicitly does. *Fix:* name the source component/metric definition for FR-16's engagement index, or flag it `[ASSUMPTION: matches existing engagement-index calculation as-is]`.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§5 Non-Goals does real work — it isn't a rubber-stamp list. "No backend/API work" is qualified with the actual reason (no prior backend design exists beyond the tutor-approval funnel), which is more honest than a bare exclusion. The `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` tag on the visual-redesign exclusion is used correctly — a real thing being deliberately deferred, not silently assumed. FR-3's Out of Scope ("Impersonating a specific real Tutor account's live data") heads off an obvious misreading of the admin preview toggle.

Open-items density is appropriately low for an internal, mostly-resolved restructuring PRD: 1 inline `[ASSUMPTION]`, 5 Assumptions Index entries, 0 open questions, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts. That's proportionate to stakes — but see Mechanical notes below on the Assumptions Index round-trip, which weakens this dimension's mechanics even though the content itself is honest.

### Findings
- **low** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts anywhere in the document — the Master/Support empty-demo-state design (FR-3) and the "decide yourself" backend-ownership call (§9, last entry) both read as judgment calls a PM would want flagged even though they're already resolved. *Fix:* not blocking given the doc is post-resolution, but consider retrofitting `[NOTE FOR PM]` at FR-3 and at the §9 "Decided by builder" entry so a PM skimming can find where they exercised delegated judgment.

## Downstream usability — strong
Glossary (§3) is substantive and each term is load-bearing: "Group Study Pool" vs. "Synchronous Study Room" is explicitly disambiguated ("Do not conflate with Group Study Pool") because the two are easy to confuse and both appear in adjacent FRs (FR-12 vs. FR-14). FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates (FR-1–19, UJ-1–2, SM-1/2/C1). Cross-references resolve: FR-3's "(FR-15–FR-19)" range, FR-2's "(see §5 Non-Goals)", FR-14's "(see §6.2, §9)" all point to real, matching content. Both UJs have named protagonists (Priya, Raj) with role, context, and a concrete multi-step path rather than a floating one-liner.

Minor casing drift: Glossary defines "Tutor Slot" (capitalized) but FR-9's body text says "tutor slots" (lowercase) — trivial, noted under Mechanical notes rather than as a dimension-level issue.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a brownfield, internal-tool restructuring, and the PRD is shaped accordingly: a capability-spec-first structure (§4 organized by existing feature area, not by invented user flow), exactly two UJs used only to anchor entry-state and role-purity behavior rather than to carry the whole spec, and no market/competitive section — all consistent with the agreed stakes. Existing-code references were spot-checked and are accurate: `features/Dashboard/`, `features/TutorHub/` (including `TutorHubView.tsx`, `StudentTutorBookingView.tsx`, `TutorEducatorHubView.tsx`), `tutorService.ts`, `groupStudyService.ts`, `userService.ts`, `scheduleService.ts`, `DEFAULT_VISIBLE_TABS`/`rolePermissions` in `App.tsx`, and `FrontEnd/docs/FRONTEND_PRD.md` §4.3/§4.6 all exist exactly as cited. New-vs-existing capability is clearly distinguished throughout (§0's "almost every capability described here already exists," §5's explicit "does not introduce any capability that doesn't already exist"). No over-formalization (no persona proliferation, no forced NFR section) and no under-formalization (the routing mechanism, the one genuinely new piece of behavior, gets full FR + Consequences treatment in §4.1).

## Mechanical notes

The other four §9 entries — role-from-`UserProfile.role` (§2.1/§4.1), the Master/Support toggle design (§2.2/§4.1 FR-3), the BACKEND_PRD authorship call (§5/§9), and Recent Activity staying static (§4.3 FR-14) — are stated as plain fact inline with no `[ASSUMPTION]` marker, even though the Index labels them "Confirmed"/"Resolved"/"Decided." This may be intentional (they're resolved decisions, not live assumptions), but if so the Index's title and the rubric's expected convention diverge — worth a one-line note in §9 clarifying that this index doubles as a decision log, not purely open assumptions.
- **Glossary casing drift:** "Tutor Slot" (§3, capitalized term-of-art) vs. "tutor slots" (§4.3 FR-9, lowercase common noun) — same referent, inconsistent case. Low-impact.
- Title page still carries "*Working title — confirm.*" (line 9) — not tagged as an open item anywhere in §8/§9, so it could be missed in a final pass.
- ID continuity: clean. FR-1–19, UJ-1–2, SM-1/SM-2/SM-C1 all contiguous, no duplicates or gaps.
