# PRD Quality Review — New Course Wizard

## Overall verdict
This is a well-constructed, launch-grade-caliber PRD: it states a real thesis, names a genuine architectural decision as the document's center of gravity (§0, §4.1), gives nearly every FR a testable consequence, and keeps brownfield reuse (MasterDataManager, DrilldownPanel.tsx, KaTeX) distinguished from new work throughout rather than only in §0. The gaps that remain are real but fixable: one brownfield integration point (`ReaderCanvas.tsx`'s fake LLM assistant) is named in the addendum but never mapped to an owning FR, the Performance NFR reverts to adjectives instead of bounds, and the MVP scope section reads as "everything" without stating why nothing was phased out. No dimension is broken; several are strong.

## Decision-readiness — strong
§0 explicitly elevates one requirement above the feature list ("This PRD's single most important requirement is architectural, not features-list") and FR-2 states the managed-now/self-hosted-at-launch decision with a named reason (data residency over student content), not smoothed to "we'll evaluate both." §4.7's UX placement note ("decided in review... but the Level 1 view must carry a visible nudge") is a decision stated as a decision, with the tension (discoverability vs. not cluttering the primary action) named rather than hidden. §5's Non-Goals — especially "No automatic personalization that adapts depth or style to a student over time without their action" — is a real, arguable product stance, not a hedge. §8's six Open Questions are genuinely unresolved (Q4 taxonomy State/City-per-Board directly gates FR-8's own acceptance criteria; Q1 auto-grading directly gates FR-19's scope), not rhetorical.

One place this slips: FR-3's cross-provider fallback ("retries on an alternate provider when the primary is rate-limited or unavailable") is framed purely as a reliability win, with no acknowledgment that a fallback model may be materially worse at reasoning-heavy tasks like `explainTopic` (Level 4/5 drill-down, per addendum's own tiering: "frontier reasoning model for Drill-Down" vs. cheaper alternatives). The PRD elsewhere is careful to route by cost/quality tier (§4.9's cheapest-tier default for `defineKeyword`) but doesn't extend that awareness to what a *degraded* fallback response looks like for a high-stakes task — a trade-off named nowhere.

### Findings
- **high** Fallback quality degradation left undiscussed (§4.1 FR-3) — FR-3's consequence only tests that "a simulated primary-provider failure results in a successful response from the configured fallback provider," with success defined purely as non-error. Given the addendum's own tiering (frontier model for Drill-Down, cheap model for keyword defs), a fallback for `explainTopic` could silently produce materially shallower Level 4/5 content than UJ-3 describes admins actively trying to fix — with no signal to admin, tutor, or student that degraded-model content was served. *Fix:* add a consequence requiring the fallback event to be logged/flagged (ties into FR-4's usage tracking) so a string of fallback-served generations is visible to the admin who configured the "stronger reasoning model" in UJ-3, and consider a [NOTE FOR PM] on whether fallback is permitted at all for `explainTopic`/`rewriteExplanation` vs. reserved for lower-stakes tasks.
- **low** Self-hosted migration trade-off stated one-directionally (§4.1 FR-2, Cross-Cutting NFRs §4.14 Portability) — the PRD names *why* to move to self-hosted (data residency) but never what's given up (addendum: managed gateways offer 300+ models via one key and zero ops burden; LiteLLM-class self-hosting means the team now owns config-file-driven routing/budgets/fallback infra). *Fix:* one sentence in §4.14 Portability naming the ops-cost trade explicitly, so the architecture pass inherits the full trade rather than just the winning side.

## Substance over theater — strong
Personas are load-bearing, not decorative: Meera drives the authoring/editing FRs (UJ-1, UJ-4), Aarav drives the adaptive-learning FRs (UJ-2), and the Admin drives the AI-configuration FRs (UJ-3) — three roles, each mapped to a distinct FR cluster, well under the four-persona ceiling. The Vision (§1) is concrete to this product ("Chapters → Topics → Subtopics → Content," "go one level deeper at a time, or try a completely different explanation") rather than swappable boilerplate — it would not paste cleanly into an unrelated e-learning PRD. No inflated novelty claims. Cross-Cutting NFRs (§4.14) are mostly specific and product-tied (cost tracking "live from day one," `defineKeyword` "default-routed to the cheapest viable tier") rather than generic "must be scalable" filler — with one exception below.

### Findings
- **medium** Performance NFR reverts to adjectives, not bounds (§4.14 Cross-Cutting NFRs, "Performance") — "near-instant response," "target low-latency response" have no numeric bound (no p95 target, no max acceptable latency for on-demand `defineKeyword` calls). This is the one NFR bullet in an otherwise specific list that reads as boilerplate. *Fix:* state a concrete target (e.g., "`defineKeyword` p95 < 1.5s" or similar) even as an `[ASSUMPTION: ... confirm before build]` — consistent with how FR-6/FR-9/FR-11 handle unspecified numeric caps elsewhere in this same PRD.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit and the features earn their place under it: tutors get AI-assisted structuring so they edit rather than author from blank (§1, FR-13/FR-14), students get depth-on-demand and style-on-demand rather than one fixed explanation (FR-17/FR-18), and both are bound by the express non-goal that the *system* never auto-selects depth or style for a student (§5). Success Metrics validate the thesis, not just activity: SM-1/SM-2 measure whether adaptive modes actually help comprehension, not just whether they're clicked, and the counter-metrics (SM-C1 against gaming extraction-acceptance with vaguer structures, SM-C2 against deliberately weakening Level 1/Way 1 to manufacture deeper engagement) are genuine adversarial checks, not decorative "we also track X."

### Findings
- **medium** MVP scope reads as the full feature list with minimal phasing rationale (§6.1 vs §6.2) — §6.1 "In Scope" includes the full AI Service Layer, full wizard, all four adaptive-learning mechanisms, pre-generation/caching, the full lifecycle, and full admin tooling; §6.2's cuts (auto-grading, non-English/Hindi languages, deep OCR tuning, bulk import) are all *secondary* refinements, not phase-outs of a primary mechanism. For a document that elsewhere is disciplined about naming trade-offs, there's no stated reason why, e.g., shipping the wizard + drill-down first and Ways/exercises in a fast-follow wasn't considered, given this is "a major new product surface" per the review brief. *Fix:* either add a sentence in §6 explaining why the four adaptive mechanisms must launch together (e.g., "Ways cannot ship without Drill-Down because UX placement in §4.7 depends on it existing as the primary action"), or flag the all-at-once scope as a [NOTE FOR PM] risk given launch-grade stakes.
- **low** No counter-metric guards SM-5 (cost per topic) against quality trade-offs — SM-C1/SM-C2 guard against gaming SM-3/SM-1, but nothing guards against hitting the SM-5 budget threshold (FR-29) by silently routing to weaker models in a way that erodes SM-2 ("I understood this"). *Fix:* an SM-C3 noting SM-5 must not be optimized by degrading model quality below what SM-2 requires — natural pairing with the FR-3 fallback-visibility finding above.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are genuinely unforgiving in the right way: FR-9's thumbnail consequences ("Upload of a 4th thumbnail is rejected," "Exactly one thumbnail is marked primary at any time"), FR-15's confirmation-reset rule, and FR-16's golden-file visual-regression requirement are all concretely testable, not aspirational. But a few spots don't survive a strict reading.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-12's descriptive claim outruns its own testable consequence (§4.3) — the description promises the OCR pass produces "clean structured text/markdown," but the only consequence given is "a scanned PDF produces a non-empty parsed-text intermediate before extraction begins." A garbled, low-fidelity OCR output would satisfy "non-empty" while failing "clean." *Fix:* either add a quality-gate consequence (e.g., a confidence-score threshold below which the file routes to failed/retry per FR-13, rather than passing through to `extractStructure()`), or soften the description to match what's actually tested.
- **low** "Secure storage" and "access control" left undefined (FR-11; Cross-Cutting NFRs §4.14 Security & Privacy) — FR-11 says files get "secure storage," and §4.14 says "stored with access control and validated/scanned before processing," but neither states the model (course-owner-only? role-based via the existing `basic auth`/policy system referenced elsewhere, e.g. FR-8's `masterdata.manage` policy pattern?) or what "scanned" means (malware scan? file-type sniffing beyond extension?). *Fix:* tie to the existing policy-gate pattern already used in FR-8 ("gated by a `masterdata.manage` policy") so this isn't the one access-control statement in the PRD without a mechanism.
- **low** Post-publish version retention has no stated bound (§4.11 FR-25) — "A prior published version remains retrievable after a new version is published" doesn't say how many versions, or whether retention is unlimited. Unlike FR-6/FR-9/FR-11's unspecified numeric caps, this one isn't flagged as an `[ASSUMPTION]` or indexed in §9. *Fix:* add an `[ASSUMPTION: retain N most recent versions / retain all — confirm before build]` tag and index entry.

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals does real, specific work (nine bullets, each naming a concrete exclusion with a pointer to why — e.g., "does not patch or extend Dashboard PRD's FR-18's 4-step shell — it fully supersedes that flow"), and §4.2 FR-8 / §4.8 FR-19 each carry an inline "Out of Scope:" callout at the exact point of relevance rather than deferring everything to the back of the document. The Assumptions Index (§9) is real and mostly round-trips correctly against inline `[ASSUMPTION: ...]` tags. Open-item density (6 Open Questions + 11 indexed assumptions across 29 FRs) is moderate for the stated stakes — not alarming, but two of the open items (FR-8's State/City-per-Board, and the language-scope question) directly gate FR-8's and FR-16's own acceptance criteria rather than being purely deferred polish, so they're higher-priority to close before story-writing than the other four.

### Findings
- **low** Assumptions Index roundtrip has two mismatches (§9 vs. body) — (1) UJ-1's inline tag `[ASSUMPTION: surfaced to Meera only as a status label, not a technical detail]` (§2.3) has no corresponding §9 entry; (2) conversely, §9 lists "§4.2 FR-9 — Exact fixed aspect ratio for thumbnail cropping not specified; recommend 16:9" but FR-9's body has no inline `[ASSUMPTION: ...]` tag on the aspect-ratio clause (only the file-size-cap clause is tagged inline). *Fix:* add the missing inline tag to FR-9 and add the missing UJ-1 entry to §9, or explicitly scope §9 to "FR-level assumptions only" if UJ-level assumptions are meant to be excluded.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary (§3) is real and terms are used identically across sections — "Way" vs. "Drill-Down Level" stays distinct everywhere it's used (FR-17 vs. FR-18, §8 Q2), "Lifecycle State" values match between the Glossary and §4.11's FR text, and none of the superseded Dashboard-PRD vocabulary (`Module`, `Lesson`) leaks into the new FRs. FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1 through FR-29 with no gaps across §4.1–§4.13; UJ-1–4; SM-1–5 plus SM-C1/C2), and cross-references resolve correctly (e.g., FR-25's "follows FR-15's small/not-small rule" and FR-15's own regeneration clause pointing to "§4.10, FR-21" both check out).

### Findings
- **medium** UJ-3's protagonist is unnamed ("An admin") while UJ-1/UJ-4 (Meera) and UJ-2 (Aarav) are named (§2.3) — the dimension's own bar is that UJs should have named protagonists, and the Admin is a first-class persona in §2.1's JTBD list with a real motivation ("so the module never depends on one vendor being up"). Leaving this one journey generic weakens it as UX source material relative to the other three. *Fix:* name the admin persona (even minimally) for consistency, since UX/story-creation will otherwise treat this journey as lower-fidelity than the other three by default.

## Shape fit — strong
This PRD earns its UJ-heavy, brownfield-aware shape: three of four journeys have named protagonists driving real feature clusters, and — notably — the brownfield distinction is sustained past §0 rather than front-loaded there. FR-8 restates the "reuses MasterDataManager, out of scope to rebuild it" boundary at the point of use; FR-16 names exactly which existing files (`ReaderCanvas.tsx`, `DrilldownPanel.tsx`, `FlashcardsModal.tsx`, `CoursePlayer.tsx`) already carry KaTeX and what's being added (`mhchem`, "not currently installed"); §5 Non-Goals reiterates all three major reuse boundaries (MasterDataManager, Dashboard FR-18 supersession, DrilldownPanel.tsx) in one place for anyone skimming only that section. This is exactly the "new-vs-existing distinguished throughout" bar the dimension asks for — and it's largely met.

One boundary is missed, though, and it's a citable one:

### Findings
- **high** `ReaderCanvas.tsx`'s existing fake LLM integration point is named in the addendum but never assigned to an owning FR in the PRD body — the addendum's "Repo prior art" section flags `ReaderCanvas.tsx`'s "simulated/fake 'LLM Assistant' (`handleAskLevelLLM`, hardcoded response, no network call) — to be replaced by real AI Service Layer calls," but no FR in `prd.md` claims this replacement. FR-16 mentions `ReaderCanvas.tsx` only in the context of KaTeX rendering reuse, not the fake-LLM affordance; §4.6 (Drill-Down) reuses `DrilldownPanel.tsx`, not `ReaderCanvas.tsx`. It's unclear whether `handleAskLevelLLM` is (a) superseded by FR-17/FR-20 and should be explicitly retired, (b) a distinct capability this PRD silently drops (and should therefore appear in §5 Non-Goals), or (c) an oversight. Since this is exactly the kind of existing-vs-new ambiguity architecture will trip over first, it belongs in the PRD, not only the addendum. *Fix:* add one sentence — either in §4.6/§4.9 (if `handleAskLevelLLM` is being subsumed by Drill-Down/Keyword-Definition) or in §5 Non-Goals (if it's explicitly out of scope/to be removed) — resolving which.
